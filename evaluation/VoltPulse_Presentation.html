VoltPulse

  • EV Charging Analytics and Business Intelligence
  • Parth Vij / S24CSU1616
  • CSET 371 / Bennett University

Three decisions around EV charging

  • Driver: find a suitable charger and plan a trip within range.
  • Operator: compare session energy and active duration.
  • Planner: examine station coverage and identify where more evidence is needed.

Where the data comes from

  • 5,000 synthetic charging sessions across four Indian cities.
  • Open Charge Map India snapshot for the public station directory.
  • Separate simulated Boulder-labelled events for telemetry replay.
  • Published Indian electricity demand provides grid context.

How VoltPulse processes the inputs

  • CSV sessions → PySpark ETL → prepared session data
  • Prepared data → scikit-learn benchmark → metrics and charts
  • Raw sessions → native Spark MLlib → terminal metrics
  • Station snapshot → web directory and planning controls

Random Forest had lower session-energy error

  • Random Forest: 7.92 kWh RMSE and R² of 0.360.
  • Gradient boosting: 8.09 kWh RMSE and R² of 0.333.
  • Same held-out split. The RMSE difference was 0.16 kWh.
  • Saved scikit-learn results for simulated sessions.

What the other three models show

  • Random Forest achieved 0.962 weighted F1 on hour-derived congestion labels.
  • Linear regression had a 16.88-minute MAE for active duration.
  • K-means grouped coordinates into four clusters with a silhouette of 0.445.
  • A centroid identifies a group of coordinates. Site feasibility requires more data.
